# Minimal portfolio direction

Build a professional, personal research portfolio with a restrained Apple-like visual language: white and soft neutrals, clear typography, generous spacing, and purposeful motion. A centered, readable composition is a starting point; the layout remains open to exploration.

Make the introduction, research contributions, and paper/contact links immediately accessible. Keep normal scrolling and a clear hierarchy. Subtle inline expansion can add depth and a little tangibility; avoid elaborate gadget metaphors, floating navigation, or interactions required to discover basic information.

The work should provide the visual personality. Include a representative interactive preview for each of the six selected papers—Stylette, Cells/Generators/Lenses, EvalLM, Evalet, CUPID, and DiscoverLLM—and static visuals for the other publications. Each preview should communicate something even untouched. Ground interactions in the real paper/system; clearly label curated examples. Avoid generic decorative demos and an almost entirely text-only page.

## References to draw from

- [Apple product pages](https://www.apple.com/macbook-air/): visual restraint, hierarchy, and generous space around meaningful imagery.
- [Fred Hohman](https://fredhohman.com/) and [Ryo Suzuki](https://ryosuzuki.org/): research portfolios with concrete project evidence and accessible publication links.
- [Bartosz Ciechanowski — Mechanical Watch](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/): small, direct interactions that reveal how the subject works. Borrow the explanatory approach at portfolio scale.
- [Ink & Switch](https://www.inkandswitch.com/) *(additional reference)*: connect research questions to tangible tools and illustrated projects.
- [Nicky Case — Nutshell](https://ncase.me/nutshell/) *(additional reference)*: optional inline expansion for secondary explanations while keeping the main reading path short.
- [Rauno Freiberg](https://rauno.me/) *(additional reference)*: study interaction craft and attention to detail for restrained finishing touches.

These are selective references, not templates to reproduce. No previous prototype is an approved direction. Start fresh and let clarity, representative visuals, and ease of browsing guide the next design.
